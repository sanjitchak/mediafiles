REEL 01

The four leaks, ranked by what they cost you

Fix one and two alone and you should clear 12%. All four together is how you reach the target.

1

You close to a call, not a purchase

Your CTA is "pay 1,999, get on the 5PM qualification call, pay the rest later." That is a second sales event you have to win again. Three steps where Dan Henry demands one: one link, one action.

2

You give away the full how-to

You teach the complete multi-angle, character-replacement and product-replacement workflows, prompts included. People leave self-sufficient. Give the aha, never the execution.

3

No single big idea, no named mechanism

You sell eight opportunities at once. Scatter equals no conviction. There is nothing for them to believe in that only you can deliver.

4

Weak open, diffused close, no real urgency

No Day 2 promise, no reason to stay, no engineered deadline. The close ends on a borrowed "life is short" video instead of one decisive instruction.

REEL 02 · CUT THESE

What you are doing wrong

Every item below is in your Day 2 script. Grouped by where the money leaks.

A

Close & offer mechanics

where most of the money is lost
01

The close is a call, not a saleTop Leak

Webinar to token to 5PM call to final payment. Each step bleeds people. For a 30k decision this roughly halves yield versus a direct close.

02

The "qualification" frame kills urgencyHigh

Refundable, plus reversible, plus "you might be rejected." You handed them a free exit at the moment you needed commitment. Nobody decides later when later is free.

03

Urgency is asserted, not engineeredMedium

"It never comes back" and "we buy tools tonight" are claims with no on-screen mechanism. No timer, no visible seat cap, no price step.

04

Three competing value anchors

2,35,000, then "tools alone are 30,000," then "83 rupees a day." They compete instead of compounding. Pick one dominant anchor.

05

The bonus values look invented

35k for a certificate, 55k for a portfolio template. A skeptical buyer divides by ten. Inflated stacks that fail a sanity check reduce trust.

06

Objections handled late and defensively

"You think your hour is worth only 300 rupees? Like seriously?" talks down to the buyer. Objections must be pre-killed inside the content, not argued after the price.

07

The charity-and-guilt line is manipulative

"By booking a call you help hungry kids and dogs on the street," dropped at the close, reads as leverage and damages trust with analytical buyers. Cut it.

08

The closing video works against you

The "life is short / live for the weekend" clip is existential, off-topic, not tied to acting now, and is borrowed copyright. Replace with a 60-second results montage and one instruction.

B

The teaching block

your second biggest leak
09

You give away the complete how-toHigh

Full workflows with copy-paste prompts. The webinar should give the what and why, never the full how. You let people leave satisfied and self-sufficient.

10

You then destroy the value of what you sell

"Students can do this in 5 to 10 minutes" and "my editing is shit." You proved the skill valuable, then proved it trivial. Pick one.

11

The content block runs far too long

Your pitch does not begin until roughly 1:49. Anik's macro is 20 to 30 minutes of content. Yours is more than double, after which energy and attendance have dropped.

C

Belief & message architecture

12

No single big ideaMedium

High-CTR creatives, thumbnails, PPM dockets, faceless channels, micro-dramas, real estate, UGC, Hollywood replacement. Dan Henry: the whole script is built on one belief.

13

You never name a mechanism

"Workflows not tools" is close but never packaged into a named, ownable thing. Without a named mechanism there is nothing only you can deliver.

14

Your three "secrets" do not break beliefs

They teach features. They should each kill one false belief: the vehicle, the internal, the external. So objections survive into the close.

15

The transformation promise is hedged

"12 viral films" is an output. The real promise (you will land paying clients) is constantly softened with "even if you don't continue with me." Hedging reads as low conviction.

D

Proof

16

Proof is told, not shown

8 lakh, a 12 lakh contract, 20 lakh MRR, 1.4 million views, all verbal and fast. At this density, unverified claims lower trust. Every number needs a screenshot.

17

Student proof has no result attached

Film clips play with no on-screen maker, price, or before-and-after. Proof without a result is just content.

E

Authority & production

small things that compound
18

You break your own authority

"Okay, fuck this," "battery's dead," "my editing is shit." Each one resets you to amateur in front of someone about to pay 30k.

19

The cancer remark reads as manipulation

Pivoting from laughing emojis to "heard about an incident of cancer, heartbreaking" is confusing, off-topic guilt leverage. Cut it entirely.

20

Live tech fumbles during the close

"I may have deleted the frames," a failing ChatGPT calculation on screen. Fumbling in the money section signals you are not in control. Pre-bake every demo.

21

Constant production checks waste energy

"Is my audio audible," "is there echo," many times. It trains the audience to watch for problems. Test before, then stop asking.

22

No "who it's for / not for" and no commitment round

You skip the free priming both templates open with: who it is for, who it is explicitly not for, and the agreements (open mind, take action, acknowledge the investment).

23

No re-hook, no reason to stay

Day 2 opens with small talk into film review. No big promise, no three reasons to stay. Your only bribe (free tool access) is buried in one line.

REEL 03 · KEEP THESE

What you are doing right

Do not touch these in the edit. They are working.

The Day 1 to Day 2 film bridge

Real output, made with you. Strong social proof in principle. Tighten it and attach a result to each clip.

The "three choices" frame

Do nothing, DIY and suffer, or do it with me. Correct and well placed.

The week-by-week walk-through

Eight weeks, each producing a real asset. Concrete and tangible. Add a proof shot beside each week.

The first-mover timing argument

Headwind and tailwind. A genuinely good "act now" frame. Move it earlier and tie it to your one big idea.

The 1:1 call as golden-goose bonus

The right instinct. Present it last and make access feel earned.

"Invest in the asset, which is you"

Solid reframe. Deliver it before the price as a belief, not after as a rebuttal.

Live cohort, not recorded

A real differentiator, said well. Lean into it harder as part of the mechanism.

REEL 04 · THE NEW RUNTIME

The fix, sequenced

Dan Henry mechanics on the Anik pacing clock. Timecodes are targets for a 90 to 110 minute Day 2.

00:00–08:00Open & re-hook

Set the stakes before anything else

  • One-sentence Day 2 big promise: the exact method to turn AI films into paying clients.
  • Three real reasons to stay to the end, stated up front and reinforced twice.
  • Who this is for. Who it is not for. This frames and filters.
  • Commitment round: open mind, take action today, acknowledge the investment. Get a "I agree."
08:00–16:00Film review as proof

Turn the review into evidence, not filler

  • Four to six clips maximum.
  • For each: show who made it, that it was their first film, and the result or use case.
  • Cut every "drop a heart" and every audio check.
16:00–24:00One idea, one mechanism

Name the thing they can only get from you

  • Single belief: winners reverse-engineer what already went viral and recreate it on demand.
  • Name the mechanism so it sounds new and ownable, for example "The Recreation Method."
  • Stop selling eight opportunities. Sell one method that unlocks many.
24:00–50:00Three secrets

Three secrets that break three beliefs (give the aha, not the how)

  • Secret 1 (vehicle): the opportunity is real and huge right now. Timing plus market size. End on a result.
  • Secret 2 (internal): you can do this without being technical. Recreation beats invention. End on a non-technical result.
  • Secret 3 (external): no clients, product, money or time is fine. Show the paths. End on a from-nothing result.
  • After each: "Who wants the done-with-you version of this?"
50:00–55:00Transition

The bridge, not a gear shift

  • "That was the what and why. The how, done with you every weekend, is next."
  • Then the three choices.
55:00–90:00Offer & close

The seven-element close, in order

  • 1 Bridge. 2 Stack by outcome, not line items. 3 Price with one dominant anchor.
  • 4 Risk reversal tied to action, not a refundable deposit. 5 Objections, already pre-killed.
  • 6 Urgency that is real and visible: seat cap or timer. 7 One step: one link, one instruction.
  • If you keep the call, rename it onboarding, not qualification. The next step for people who are in, not an interview they can fail.
90:00–ENDGolden goose & push

Land the plane

  • Reveal the 1:1 call last, framed as earned.
  • 60-second student-results montage, then one instruction. Stop.
  • Q&A until no sales for five minutes, then close. No "life is short" video.
REEL 05 · LINE EDITS

Before and after, in your own moments

The three highest-leverage rewrites, swappable straight into the script.

The Price Reveal
× Current

"Not 2,35,000, not 1,25,000, just 29,999 plus GST. Pay 1,999, get on the qualification call, money back if you don't qualify."

✓ Rewrite

"The tools alone cost 30,000 a month if you bought them yourself. The cohort, the method, the team, a year of access: 29,999, one time. We take [X] this batch and [N] seats are gone. The button is on your screen. One click, you are in, you start this weekend."

Urgency
× Current

"Decide before 5PM, we buy the tools tonight."

✓ Rewrite

A real seat counter or timer on the slide. "This batch closes at [time] or when the last seat goes. After that the next batch is [date] at [higher price]." Say it only if it is true. Fake urgency is worse than none.

The Closing Asset
× Current

The borrowed "life is short, you'll live for the weekend" YouTube video.

✓ Rewrite

A 60-second cut of student wins (the 12 lakh jewelry contract, the 2 lakh music-video project, the viral reel) with on-screen proof, ending on your single instruction.
